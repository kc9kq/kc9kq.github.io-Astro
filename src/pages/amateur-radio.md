## Amateur Radio

Amateur — “ham” — radio is a licensed service for experimentation and communication across an enormous range of bands and modes. It’s where you go when you want to build, tinker, and reach further than any consumer radio allows.

Yes, it takes a test — but the entry-level Technician exam is very approachable: 35 multiple-choice questions drawn from a published pool, and most people pass after just 10–20 hours of study with free tools. No Morse code required. [Lifted from RFLab.io: Ham Radio](https://rflab.io/topics/ham-radio.html)

### Operating

As a licensed operator, you are assigned a call sign. You use that to identify yourself while talking or keying on air. The two most common ways of communication are Phone (voice) and (optionally) CW (Continuous Wave/Morse Code). An Additional method is via Digitial (dat).

### Getting a License

To get a license and a call sign, you will need to pass the Technician exam. Additional exams that you can take are the General and the Amatuer Extra. Each grants you access to slices of the radio spectrum (Bands). Technician grants you VHF, UHF, and a slice of HF. General gives you most of the HF spectrum, Amatuer Extra gives you the rest.  Exams are regularly proctored in the area. Contact one of us for more information and where to start/go next.

### Hardware

You will need a base station transciever and an antenna - also if necessary a tuner and amplifier. There are a wide variety of off-the-shelf stations and antenna with connection cables available. You can also make your own antenna. Strategicly mount and tune the antenna to connect with repeaters and optimum contact finding. A TV tower is ideal, but not everyone has one nowadays, so mounting to a roof or a supported pole are good alternatives. Take care to protect your equipment from weather. Our club members are available to help out with installing your equipment

### Beetown Repeater

The club sponsors and operates the Beetown, WI 2-meter band (VHF) to support local conversation over the air as well as various nets

### Hospital Repeater

The Club also sponsors and operates the Southwest Health Hospital 70 cm band (UHF) repeater. This repeater serves as a low-power connection to the Beetown repeater for handheld radios (handie talkies) which cannot transmit the power needed to connect to Beetown directly.

repeater connection tuning
444.325 MHz, Offset - 5.0 MHz, PL Tone 131.8 Hz
